Hello, I'm Kevin.

I grew up a military brat, moving every two or three years, eleven of them in Germany. You learn to read a room fast when you are always the new kid. The first thing I remember building was not software. In middle school I ran a Coca-Cola business out of my backpack, and I started a Minecraft club that ended up with more than forty students staying after school to play together. That club is the one I still think about. I was not the best player in it. What I was good at was figuring out why people showed up, keeping the thing running, and making it worth their time to come back on Tuesday. Between that and years of varsity wrestling and soccer, I spent my whole adolescence learning how groups work.

I studied Information Technology at UCF, in the College of Engineering and Computer Science, and finished in December 2025. The coursework was technical, data structures and machine learning and robot vision, but the part that kept pulling at me was the question underneath the code. Who is this actually for, and does it help them.

That question is why I started Loop. There was no student organization at UCF built around product management, so I made one. I recruited an officer team, ran workshops on product lifecycle, user research, and AI tools, and brought in speakers from NBCUniversal, Disney, and Google. More than sixty students came through it. We also shipped the club's own website, which meant I spent that year translating Figma mockups and API data into Jira sprints while teaching other people to do the same thing.

Professionally I have moved back and forth between the product side and the data side, and that combination is the most useful thing about me. At NBCUniversal I scoped Fandango FanClub's MVP and led user research with more than a thousand participants for a SportsEngine mobile redesign, then joined the data science team to tune machine learning models and automate prediction pipelines across 3,500 facilities. At Kogna.IO, an AI startup, I helped launch Nodes, a modular workspace. At Disney I have worked on both guest experience analytics and product strategy, most recently authoring the requirements for a Streamlit forecasting tool covering more than forty characters across four parks.

Outside of assigned work, I build. Right now that means two products of my own. Arrival builds a language learning plan scoped to one specific trip, prioritizing the words and situations you will actually run into rather than trying to teach you a whole language. Hindsight is a regret database for travelers, where people log what they wish they had done differently and the next person reads the pattern before they go.

The short version is on the timeline. If you would rather talk, I am easy to reach.
